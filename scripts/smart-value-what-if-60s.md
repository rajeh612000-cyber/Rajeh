# Smart Value™ Pricing Optimization — "What If" (60s social film)

Format: hybrid. On camera → V/O with motion graphics → on camera close.
Platform: LinkedIn primary (1:1 square master, 4:5 and 9:16 cutdowns).
Spoken word count: 137. Target runtime: 58 seconds.
Audience: RGM, Pricing, Commercial Excellence, Category, Trade, Finance, Insights.

---

## THE SCRIPT

### 1. HOOK — ON CAMERA (0:00–0:08)

**ON CAMERA**
> "What if you put 8% on your best-selling SKU?
>
> Someone in the room will say revenue goes up.
>
> Will it?"

**VISUAL / DIRECTION**
Open on the speaker mid-thought. No logo, no greeting, no company intro.
Tight to medium shot, shallow depth, office or neutral studio.
Cut on "Will it?" to a hard beat of silence, half a second, before the graphics start.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT (0:00)**
`+8% on your best seller.`
Large, top third, up on frame one so the video reads with sound off.

---

### 2. COMPLICATE THE DECISION — V/O + ANIMATION (0:08–0:31)

**VOICE-OVER**
> "Depends who's standing in the aisle.
>
> Some pay it and never notice.
>
> Some drop to the smaller pack. You kept the shopper and lost the value.
>
> A few walk one shelf across to the competitor.
>
> And a few just stop buying the category.
>
> Same decision, four different answers, and your forecast only modelled one."

**VISUAL / DIRECTION**
Build one shelf graphic and keep it on screen for the whole section. Everything happens on that shelf.

- "Depends who's standing in the aisle" — the shelf draws in. A single price tag flips from 100 to 108.
- "Some pay it" — one shopper path goes straight to the SKU. Line stays green, volume bar barely moves.
- "smaller pack" — path bends down the shelf to a smaller pack of the same brand. Volume bar holds, value bar drops. Label the drop on screen.
- "one shelf across" — path leaves the brand block into a competitor block. Colour changes to the competitor's. Let this one land hardest, it is the expensive one.
- "stop buying the category" — path fades out of frame entirely. Brief silence in the mix.
- "four different answers" — the four paths replay simultaneously, fast, over the same shelf. Four outcome tiles resolve at once: volume, revenue, margin, share.
- "only modelled one" — three of the four tiles grey out. One stays lit.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
`Pays it` · `Trades down` · `Switches brand` · `Leaves the category`
Then: `One price change. Four P&Ls.`

---

### 3. SMART VALUE — V/O + PRODUCT VISUAL (0:31–0:49)

**VOICE-OVER**
> "Smart Value runs the question before you commit.
>
> Move the price. Watch volume, margin and the portfolio move with it.
>
> See where the switching goes. See where the shopper stops paying.
>
> Test it on screen, not in the market."

**VISUAL / DIRECTION**
Hand off from the illustrated shelf to real product UI. Keep the same colour language so it feels like the same argument, not a new chapter.

- "runs the question" — the +8% scenario from the hook appears as a scenario row. Same number, closing the loop.
- "Move the price" — a slider moves. Outputs recalculate live. Do not cut away during the recalculation, the whole point is that the numbers move together.
- "the portfolio move with it" — pull back to the full SKU list. Two or three neighbouring SKUs move in the opposite direction. Cannibalisation, shown, not narrated.
- "where the switching goes" — switching flows between brands.
- "where the shopper stops paying" — a willingness to pay curve with the threshold marked. Land on the cliff edge.
- "not in the market" — three scenarios stack side by side: +4%, +8%, +12%. Different winners. Hold two seconds. This is the money frame.

**ON-SCREEN TEXT**
`Simulate. Then decide.`

---

### 4. CLOSE — ON CAMERA (0:49–0:58)

**ON CAMERA**
> "So the question was never 'can we raise the price.'
>
> It's 'what happens when we do.'
>
> Simulate the decision. Then take it."

**VISUAL / DIRECTION**
Back to the opening framing, same lighting, slightly wider. Speaker is calmer here than in the hook.
Hold a full beat on the face after the last word before the end card.

**END CARD (0:56–0:58)**
`Smart Value™ Pricing Optimization`
`Before you change the price, simulate the decision.`

## ALTERNATIVE HOOKS

**A. As written.** "What if you put 8% on your best-selling SKU? Someone in the room will say revenue goes up. Will it?"
Keeps the brief's what-if framing and turns it into a challenge rather than a riddle.

**B. The meeting.** "Someone has already asked for 8% on your biggest SKU. You have a week to answer."
Drops the viewer into a deadline instead of a hypothetical. Strongest for RGM and Finance, who live in this meeting.

**C. The cost of being right.** "Eight percent on your best seller. The model says yes. The model has never met your shopper."
Sharpest scroll-stopper, slightly more confrontational. Test this one if reach is the priority.

## ALTERNATIVE CLOSERS

**A. As written.** "So the question was never 'can we raise the price.' It's 'what happens when we do.'"

**B. Plainer.** "Everyone can raise a price. The job is knowing what it costs you."
Avoids the isn't/it's construction if the delivery feels too written on the day.

**C. Shortest.** "Any of us can change a price. Simulate it first."

## PRODUCTION NOTES

**Sound off is the default.** LinkedIn's own research puts a majority of mobile B2B video viewing on mute, and captioned video holds viewers roughly a third longer. Burn in captions, do not rely on auto-captions. Every number in section 2 and 3 needs an on-screen label that carries the argument alone.

**Aspect ratio.** Cut a 1:1 square master for LinkedIn. Desktop is still a large share of LinkedIn impressions and crops 4:5 badly. Produce 9:16 only for Instagram and TikTok cutdowns.

**Delivery.** Script the on-camera lines for structure, then give the speaker keyword bullets rather than a teleprompter. The hook and the closer should sound argued, not recited. Record three takes of the hook at different energy levels.

**Pace.** 137 words across 58 seconds is deliberately unhurried. The pauses are part of the argument. Do not fill the gap after "Will it?" with music.

**Numbers.** Everything on screen is illustrative. If a real client scenario can be cleared for use, swap it in and add a source line to the end card. Real numbers beat a good line.

**Cutdowns from the same shoot.**
- 15s paid: hook, the competitor switch beat, end card.
- 30s: hook, section 2, end card.
- Static carousel: the four shopper outcomes, one per card.
