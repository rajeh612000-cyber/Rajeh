A few weeks ago I sat down in Amsterdam with two people I don't get to talk to nearly enough.

Sami Ghabrial

Founded Marketeers Research and is its CEO. He has spent 32 years watching FMCG companies make pricing and promotion decisions, and the firm's work across global markets spans those same 32 years.

 

Ravneet Singh

Leads Global Pricing at RHI Magnesita. Before that, years spent building pricing functions across India, the Middle East and Europe.

No slides, no plan for the conversation really. We just started talking about what actually changes when AI shows up in a commercial team.

Ravneet said something I've been chewing on since.

 

You can have good data, a strong strategy, the right technology, and still watch it all go nowhere if the people who have to use it don't trust it.

RAVNEET SINGH

Sami put a name on the same thing from his side. He calls it the human premium: the model can move faster than any team ever could, but none of that speed means anything unless someone still owns the decision at the end of it.

Two people, two very different careers, saying versions of the same thing. I keep seeing it everywhere now that I know to look for it. The teams handling this well aren't the ones with the best tool. They're the ones who worked out how they wanted to decide things first, and let the tool fit around that.

 

Curious about your team: who actually owns the call once the model has thrown in its two cents? Reply and tell me.
