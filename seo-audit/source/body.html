Section 4

Confirmed issues, with fixes and hours

Fifteen issues. Each card has the same four parts. Hours are for a competent WordPress developer or SEO who knows the site; add roughly 30% for someone seeing it for the first time.

C1 · A subdomain Google still lists no longer exists

Critical2–3 hoursDeveloper + whoever manages DNS (GoDaddy)
What we found

Google shows https://onlinepanel.marketeersresearch.com/ ("Online Panel in Middle East And Africa | Marketeers Research") in results. The DNS phone book has no entry at all for that name, so anyone who clicks gets a browser error, not even a "page not found" from your site.

Why it matters

Imagine Google handing customers a business card with an address that leads to an empty lot. Every click is a lost lead and a trust signal against you. Google also slowly drops the page, taking any links and history it had earned with it.

How to fix it
  1. Decide: do you still want a separate online-panel site? If yes, restore it (step 2). If no, redirect it (step 3). Redirecting is usually the right answer because the content already lives at /blog/audience-panel/.
  2. Restore: in GoDaddy DNS add an A record for onlinepanel pointing to the panel's server, then confirm the site loads over https with a valid certificate.
  3. Redirect: in GoDaddy DNS add an A record for onlinepanel pointing at the same Sucuri address as the main site (192.124.249.83). In Sucuri (Settings → Domains) add onlinepanel.marketeersresearch.com as an alias. Then add a permanent (301) redirect rule so that everything on the subdomain goes to https://marketeersresearch.com/blog/audience-panel/ (or a proper "Online Panel" service page if you create one). In WordPress the free plugin "Redirection" can do this.
  4. In Google Search Console, add the subdomain as a property and use "Removals" if you want it gone faster.
How you know it is fixed

Type onlinepanel.marketeersresearch.com into a browser: it should land on a real page on your site, with the padlock icon, in under a second.

My judgement: a dead subdomain in Google results is the single most embarrassing thing a research company can show a client who Googles them. Do this first, today if possible.

C5 · An insecure "http://" address is indexed, and a blog post lives outside /blog/

Critical2–4 hoursDeveloper
What we found

Google lists http://marketeersresearch.com/the-future-of-brand-tracking-is-now-dynamic-dashboard/, with plain http, not https. If your redirect from http to https were airtight, Google would have replaced this long ago. The same post also lives at the site root instead of under /blog/ like your other posts, so you have two blog-address patterns.

Why it matters

http is the unlocked front door. Browsers show "Not secure", Google prefers https versions, and if http pages answer normally you have two copies of the whole site competing with each other.

How to fix it
  1. Ask your developer to test these four addresses with a redirect checker (for example httpstatus.io): http://marketeersresearch.com/, http://www.marketeersresearch.com/, https://www.marketeersresearch.com/ and the http blog address above. Every one must answer 301 → https://marketeersresearch.com/... in a single hop.
  2. If any does not: in Sucuri turn on "Force HTTPS" (Settings → Security → Protocol Redirection), and in WordPress set both "WordPress Address" and "Site Address" to https://marketeersresearch.com. Add a fallback rule in .htaccess: 
    RewriteEngine On
RewriteCond %{HTTPS} off [OR]
RewriteCond %{HTTP_HOST} ^www\. [NC]
RewriteRule ^ https://marketeersresearch.com%{REQUEST_URI} [L,R=301]
  3. Move the stray post under the blog: change its permalink to /blog/the-future-of-brand-tracking-is-now-dynamic-dashboard/ and add a 301 from the old root address. Check for any other posts published at the root and do the same.
  4. Make sure every page's canonical tag (Section 5, U5) says https, never http.
How you know it is fixed

All four test addresses show exactly one 301 to the https, non-www version. In Search Console, "Pages" shows no http addresses as indexed after a few weeks.

C2 · The "Partner with Us" page exists twice, once with a typo

High0.5–1 hourAnyone with WordPress admin
What we found

Both /parnter-with-us/ (misspelled) and /partner-with-us/ are indexed with the identical title "Partner with Us - Marketeers Research".

Why it matters

  2. Add a 301: source /parnter-with-us/ → target /partner-with-us/.

  4. Search your menus, footer, and any email signatures or LinkedIn posts for the misspelled link and update them.

Typing the misspelled address instantly lands on the correct one, and the address bar shows the correct spelling.

C4 · Job postings are duplicated, scattered and stale

High2–3 hoursDeveloper + HR
What we found

"CATI Supervisor" is indexed at /jobs/cati-supervisor and /cati-supervisor-2/ (the "-2" means WordPress found the slug already taken). "Digital Marketing Specialist" sits at the site root. The CATI post is dated June 2023 but is still live and indexed as if current.

C6 · Testimonials leak through a query-string address with an outdated title

High1–2 hoursDeveloper
What we found

Google indexes marketeersresearch.com/?testimonials=sherif-banhawy-department-manager-... with the title "Marketeers – Analytics . Insights . Impact". That is a WordPress "testimonials" content type that was never meant to have its own page, and the title is an old site tagline that no longer matches your brand name elsewhere.

Why it matters

Query-string addresses look like spam to Google and users, they are thin (one quote per page), and the old tagline title muddles what your company is called. There may be one such address per testimonial.

How to fix it
  1. Ask the developer to set the testimonial post type to publicly_queryable => false and exclude_from_search => true (in the theme, the Elementor/testimonial plugin settings, or a small snippet).
  2. In Yoast (SEO → Settings → Content types → Testimonials) or Rank Math (Titles & Meta → Testimonials) set "Show in search results: No" and remove them from the XML sitemap.
  3. 301 the known query addresses to the page where the testimonials are displayed (probably the homepage or About).
  4. Update the stale site tagline in Settings → General so no template can print "Analytics . Insights . Impact" again unless you want it.

C7 · Date and category archives are indexed with duplicate or default titles

High1–2 hoursAnyone with WordPress admin
What we found

/blog/2026/02/ is indexed with the exact homepage title. /blog/category/featured/ is indexed as "Featured Archives - Marketeers Research", WordPress's default wording.

Why it matters

A month archive is just a list of links; it has nothing to say to a searcher, and giving it the homepage title makes Google think you have two homepages. Category pages can be useful, but only with a real title and a short intro.

How to fix it
  1. Yoast: SEO → Settings → Advanced → Date archives → Disable. Rank Math: Titles & Meta → Misc Pages → Date Archives → "Disable" (or noindex).
  2. Also noindex author archives (a one-author blog does not need them) and the media/attachment pages (redirect attachments to the parent).
  3. For each category you keep ("Featured" and any others), write a real title, such as "Featured Insights on FMCG Growth | Marketeers Research", and a two-sentence intro at the top of the category page.
  4. Remove "Archives" from the archive title template.

C8 · Four different brand suffixes and three different company names in titles

High3–5 hoursSEO or marketing lead, with WordPress admin
What we found

Across the indexed pages, titles end in "| Marketeers Research", "- Marketeers Research", "– Marketeers", "- Marketeers", or have no brand at all ("Advanced Analytics (Smart Shopper™)", most blog posts). One page calls you "Marketeers – Analytics . Insights . Impact".

Why it matters

Google builds a picture of your company from repeated, consistent signals. Five different endings tell it five slightly different stories. It also looks untidy when several of your pages appear together in results. There are also other companies called "Marketeers" (a marketing agency at marketeers.tech, and amarketeers.org), so consistency is how Google keeps you apart from them.

How to fix it
  1. Choose the pattern: Page name | Marketeers Research. The pipe with "Marketeers Research" is already used on your best pages.
  2. Yoast: SEO → Settings → Site basics → set Site name to "Marketeers Research" and separator to "|". Rank Math: Titles & Meta → Global → Separator "|". Then in Content types set every default template to %%title%% %%sep%% %%sitename%%.
  3. Open each page that has a custom title (Smart Shopper, Data Analytics, ZeroTime, Client's Advisory Team, Blog, every blog post) and either clear the custom title so the template applies, or rewrite it in the same pattern.
  4. Settings → General → Site Title = "Marketeers Research"; Tagline = "Guiding decisions for growth" (your approved slogan) rather than the old one.
  5. Add WebSite structured data with name: "Marketeers Research" and an alternate name "Marketeers", so Google's "site name" in results is right (Yoast and Rank Math do this automatically once the site name is set).

C10 · Trailing slash is inconsistent

Medium1–2 hoursDeveloper
What we found

Most addresses end with "/" (/careers/), but some do not (/careers-job-openings, /jobs/cati-supervisor).

To Google, /page and /page/ are two different addresses. If both answer with content, every page can quietly become a duplicate.

C13 · Google Search Console is not connected (to this audit, and possibly to anyone)

Medium1–2 hoursSite owner
Why it matters

Search Console is Google's free dashboard that tells you, every day, which pages it could not crawl, which are duplicates, which are slow, and what people searched for. Running a website without it is driving without a dashboard. Every check in Section 5 becomes a two-minute read once it is connected.

How to fix it
  1. Go to search.google.com/search-console, sign in with the company Google account, "Add property" → "Domain" → marketeersresearch.com.
  2. Google gives you a TXT record. Paste it into GoDaddy DNS (Domain → DNS → Add → TXT). Click Verify (can take a few minutes).
  3. Sitemaps → submit https://marketeersresearch.com/sitemap_index.xml (Yoast) or /sitemap_index.xml / /sitemap.xml (Rank Math). See U2.
  4. Add your SEO advisor (or this Claude workspace, Appendix B) as a user with "Full" permission.
  5. Also add the site to Bing Webmaster Tools (it imports from Search Console in one click).

Section 5

The A-to-Z checklist: 24 checks to run on the live site

These are the things a normal audit sees by loading the site, which our workspace could not do. Each one is a five-minute check you or your developer can run. If a check passes, tick it and move on. If it fails, the fix and the hours are already written. Run them all in one sitting (about 3–4 hours including note-taking), ideally after C13 (Search Console) is done, because Search Console answers half of them for you.

Two free tools that do most of this for you

  • Screaming Frog SEO Spider (free up to 500 pages, Windows/Mac). Type your domain, press Start, and it lists every page's title, description, H1, canonical, status code, image alt text and more in a spreadsheet. This one tool covers U2, U5, U6, U7, U10, U12, U13, U15, U18, U19.
  • Google PageSpeed Insights (pagespeed.web.dev). Paste an address; it grades speed, mobile, accessibility and a basic SEO list. Covers U8, U9, and flags U15.

A · Can Google get in? (Crawling)

U1 · robots.txt (the house rules on the front door)

Check0.5–1 h if wrong

Check: open https://marketeersresearch.com/robots.txt in a browser.
Good looks like: a short text file that allows everything except /wp-admin/, and ends with a Sitemap: line pointing to your XML sitemap.
Bad looks like: "Disallow: /" (blocks everything), no file at all, or blocking /wp-content/ (blocks your images and styles, which breaks mobile rendering).
Fix: Yoast → Tools → File editor, or Rank Math → General → Edit robots.txt. Paste:

User-agent: *
Disallow: /wp-admin/
Allow: /wp-admin/admin-ajax.php
Disallow: /?s=
Disallow: /*?testimonials=
Sitemap: https://marketeersresearch.com/sitemap_index.xml

U2 · XML sitemap (the map you hand to the visitor)

Check1–2 h if wrong

Check: open /sitemap_index.xml, then /sitemap.xml, then /wp-sitemap.xml. One of them should load.
Good looks like: lists only real, indexable pages and posts, all https, none of the duplicates from Section 4, no testimonials, no attachments, no date archives, and last-modified dates that are real.
Fix: turn on the Yoast/Rank Math sitemap, untick testimonials, media and dates, submit in Search Console (C13). Sitemaps do not make Google index pages, but they make the crawl faster and reveal errors in Search Console's "Sitemaps" report.

U3 · One address for everything (http, www, capital letters, index.php)

Check1–2 h if wrong

Check: at httpstatus.io paste these six: http://marketeersresearch.com, http://www.marketeersresearch.com, https://www.marketeersresearch.com, https://marketeersresearch.com/index.php, https://marketeersresearch.com/About-Us/, https://marketeersresearch.com/about-us.
Good looks like: each shows exactly one 301 straight to the final https, lower-case, trailing-slash address. Chains of two or three redirects waste crawl budget and leak a little authority each hop.
Fix: as in C5 and C10.

U4 · Noindex tags where they should not be

Check0.5 h if wrong

Check: on each key page, right-click → "View page source" → Ctrl+F for noindex. It must not appear on any service page, About, Contact, Blog or blog posts.
Why: a single accidental "noindex" (often left on after a site rebuild, or set by "Discourage search engines" in Settings → Reading) makes a page vanish from Google. This is the most common catastrophic mistake we see after redesigns.
Fix: untick Settings → Reading → "Discourage search engines"; clear per-page noindex in Yoast/Rank Math.

B · Which copy is the real one? (Duplication)

U5 · Canonical tags

Check1–2 h if wrong

Check: view source on five pages and find <link rel="canonical".
Good looks like: the canonical is the page's own clean https address (self-referencing). On the duplicate pages from Section 4 it should point to the survivor.
Bad: missing, pointing to http, pointing to the homepage on every page (a known theme bug), or two canonicals.
Fix: Yoast/Rank Math add correct canonicals automatically; remove any hard-coded ones from the theme header.

U6 · Meta descriptions

Check4–6 h to write for ~40 pages

Check: Screaming Frog → "Meta Description" tab: missing, duplicate, over 160 characters.
Why: the grey text under the blue link. It does not affect ranking directly, but it decides whether people click. Google writes its own if yours is missing, and its version is usually worse.
Fix: one unique 140–155-character description per page: what the page offers, for whom, and a reason to click. Blog posts can use the first paragraph.

U7 · Headings (H1, H2, H3)

Check3–5 h if wrong

Check: Screaming Frog → H1 tab. Each page needs exactly one H1 that says what the page is about. Elementor sites often have zero H1s (the logo or a decorative block is set as H1) or four H1s (every section heading).
Fix: in Elementor, set the page's main heading to H1 and the section headings to H2; sub-points to H3. Never pick heading levels by font size; pick by meaning and style them separately.

C · Is it fast and mobile-friendly? (Performance)

U8 · Core Web Vitals and page speed

Check8–16 h typical for an Elementor site

Check: pagespeed.web.dev for the homepage, one service page (Smart Value) and one blog post, on Mobile. Also Search Console → "Core Web Vitals".
Good looks like: LCP (time until the biggest thing on screen appears) under 2.5 s, INP (how quickly the page reacts to a tap) under 200 ms, CLS (how much things jump around) under 0.1. A mobile Performance score of 70+ is realistic for a page-builder site; 90+ is excellent.
Why: Google uses these as a ranking tie-breaker and, more importantly, slow pages lose visitors before they read a word. Egyptian and Saudi mobile networks are less forgiving than a Cairo office Wi-Fi.
Fix, in order of return on effort:

  1. Compress and resize images; serve WebP (plugin: ShortPixel, Imagify or EWWW). Usually the single biggest win. (2–4 h)
  2. Caching and minification: WP Rocket (paid, easiest) or LiteSpeed/W3TC, plus Sucuri's caching set to "Enabled (recommended)". (2–3 h)
  3. Load fonts locally, limit to two families, and preload the hero image. (1–2 h)
  4. Remove unused plugins and Elementor add-ons; every plugin adds scripts to every page. (2–4 h)
  5. Defer non-critical JavaScript (chat widgets, analytics) and lazy-load videos. (1–3 h)

U9 · Mobile-friendliness

Check2–4 h if wrong

Check: open the site on a phone. Do you need to pinch-zoom? Do buttons overlap? Is the menu usable? Also view source for <meta name="viewport".
Why: Google ranks the mobile version of your site, not the desktop one, for everyone.
Fix: Elementor → responsive mode → adjust font sizes, padding and column stacking for tablet and mobile; add the viewport meta if missing (any modern theme has it).

U10 · Images: alt text, size, file names

Check4–8 h for a full pass

Check: Screaming Frog → Images tab: "Missing Alt Text" and "Over 100 KB".
Why: alt text is how Google (and blind users) understand a picture; heavy images are the number one cause of slow pages.
Fix: Media Library → add a short description to every meaningful image ("Marketeers Research retail audit dashboard, Egypt"); decorative images get an empty alt. Rename files before upload ("smart-value-dashboard.png" not "IMG_2231.png"). Compress as in U8.

D · Is it safe and trustworthy? (Security)

U11 · Certificate, HSTS and mixed content

Check1–2 h if wrong

Check: click the padlock in the browser: certificate should be valid and cover both the bare domain and www. Run ssllabs.com/ssltest (want an A). Open any page and press F12 → Console: no "Mixed Content" warnings (an https page loading an http image or script).
Fix: renew or reissue the certificate in Sucuri; run the "Better Search Replace" plugin to change http://marketeersresearch.com to https:// in the database; add the HSTS header in Sucuri → Settings → Headers.

U12 · Broken links and the 404 page

Check3–5 h

Check: Screaming Frog → Response Codes → "Client Error (4xx)" lists every broken link on the site. Then visit marketeersresearch.com/this-page-does-not-exist: it must show a friendly "page not found" page with your menu, search and a link home, and the response code must be 404 (not 200, not a redirect to the homepage).
Why: broken links are dead ends for visitors and Google; a 404 that returns "200 OK" ("soft 404") tells Google that thousands of nonsense addresses are real pages.
Fix: update or 301 every broken link; design a proper 404 template in Elementor Theme Builder.

E · Does Google understand the pages? (Structure and meaning)

U13 · Internal links and orphan pages

Check4–6 h

Check: Screaming Frog → Crawl Analysis → "Orphan pages" (pages in the sitemap that no other page links to) and "Inlinks" count per page. Every service page (Smart Value, Smart Shopper, ZeroTime, Data Analytics, Market Assessment) should be reachable within two clicks of the homepage and be linked from relevant blog posts with descriptive words, not "click here".
Why: links are the corridors of the house. Rooms without corridors do not get visited and do not get ranked.
Fix: add a "Related solutions" block to each blog post, link services to each other, and add breadcrumbs (Yoast/Rank Math have a breadcrumb block for Elementor).

U14 · Structured data (schema)

Check4–8 h

Check: paste the homepage, a blog post and the Contact page into search.google.com/test/rich-results and validator.schema.org.
Good looks like: Organization + WebSite on the homepage; Article with author, dates and image on posts; BreadcrumbList everywhere; FAQPage where you have Q&A; JobPosting on open roles; no errors.
Why: schema is a label on each room telling Google, in its own language, what is inside. It unlocks richer results and is the raw material AI search engines quote.
Fix: Yoast/Rank Math produce most of it once C8 and C15 are set; add FAQ blocks to service pages.

U15 · Open Graph and social preview tags

Check1–2 h

Check: paste a page into linkedin.com/post-inspector and opengraph.xyz. Each page should show a title, description and a 1200×630 image (your brand system already defines a 1191×595 post format; make a 1200×630 export of it).
Why: LinkedIn is your main channel; a link with no picture gets a fraction of the clicks.
Fix: Yoast/Rank Math → Social → set a default image; per-page image for key services.

U16 · Language, region and hreflang

Check0 h if single-language · a project if you add Arabic

Check: view source: <html lang="en"> should be present. Is there an Arabic version of the site? If yes, each page needs hreflang tags pairing en and ar versions (WPML or Polylang do this).
Judgement: you sell in Egypt and KSA. An Arabic version of the five service pages is one of the highest-return SEO investments available to you, but it is content work, not a bug. Do the technical fixes first, then plan it (roughly 30–50 hours including translation).

U17 · JavaScript rendering (can Google see the words?)

Check2–4 h if wrong

Check: in Search Console → URL Inspection → "Test live URL" → "View tested page" → Screenshot and HTML. Compare with what you see in a browser. Alternatively, disable JavaScript in Chrome DevTools and reload; the main text and links must still be there.
Why: Google reads the raw page first and runs scripts later, sometimes days later. Text that only appears after scripts (some sliders, tabs, "load more" buttons, pop-ups holding your key message) is invisible at first pass.
Fix: keep primary text in normal Elementor text widgets; avoid putting services only inside tabs or accordions that hide content from the raw HTML.

F · WordPress housekeeping

U18 · Archives, feeds and parameters WordPress creates by itself

Check1–2 h

Check1 h

U20 · Updates, plugins and the login door

Check2–4 h

Check: Dashboard → Updates: WordPress core, theme, Elementor and every plugin current? Plugins → any inactive or unused ones? Users → any old staff accounts with admin rights?

Fix: update everything after a backup (UpdraftPlus), delete unused plugins and themes, remove old users, enforce two-factor login (Wordfence or Sucuri plugin), rename the login address.

Check0.5–1 h

Section 7

Glossary, in plain words

SEO

Search Engine Optimisation: making it easy for Google to find, understand and recommend your site. Technical SEO is the plumbing: addresses, speed, security, structure. It is not about writing more articles.

Crawling

Google's robot walking through your house, room by room, following the corridors (links).

Indexing

Google writing down what it saw so it can show it in results. "Indexed" = in Google's notebook.

URL / address / slug

The web address of a page. The slug is the last part: in /blog/audience-panel/ the slug is audience-panel.

301 redirect

A permanent "we moved, go here instead" sign. It passes almost all ranking power to the new address. A 302 is temporary and passes less; always use 301 for moves.

Duplicate content

The same room reachable through two doors. Google has to guess which door is the main one, and often guesses wrong.

Canonical tag

A small label inside a page saying "the official address of this page is X". It is how you tell Google which door is the main one.

noindex

A label saying "do not put this page in your notebook". Useful for thank-you pages, archives and internal tools. Catastrophic on a service page.

robots.txt

A text file with house rules for robots: which corridors they may not enter.

XML sitemap

A map you hand to Google listing every room worth visiting and when it last changed.

Title tag

The blue clickable headline in Google results and the words in the browser tab. About 60 characters fit.

Meta description

The grey sentence under the blue link. About 155 characters fit.

H1, H2, H3

Headline levels inside a page. One H1 (the page's name), several H2s (sections), H3s under them. Google uses them as a table of contents.

HTTPS / SSL certificate

The padlock. Proves the site is yours and encrypts what visitors type. "http" without the "s" is the unlocked door.

DNS

The internet's phone book: it turns marketeersresearch.com into the number of the computer that hosts it. Subdomains (onlinepanel.) each need their own entry.

Subdomain

A separate "wing" of your domain, like onlinepanel.marketeersresearch.com. Google treats it almost like a separate site.

CDN / firewall (Sucuri)

A guard and a fast courier standing in front of your server: blocks attacks and serves copies of your pages from nearby locations.

Core Web Vitals

Google's three speed-and-comfort measurements: how fast the main content appears (LCP), how quickly the page reacts to taps (INP), how much things jump around while loading (CLS).

Structured data / schema

Labels in Google's own language stuck on each room: "this is an Organization", "this is an Article by X on date Y".

hreflang

A label saying "this page also exists in Arabic at address Z", so Google shows the right language to the right person.

Crawl budget

The limited time Google's robot spends in your house per visit. Duplicates and junk addresses spend it on nothing.

Search Console

Google's free dashboard telling you exactly what its robot saw, could not see, and what people searched to find you.

Query string

The part of an address after a question mark, such as /?testimonials=.... Fine for filters and searches, bad as the only address of a page.

Orphan page

A room with no corridor leading to it. It exists, but nobody, including Google, can walk in.
