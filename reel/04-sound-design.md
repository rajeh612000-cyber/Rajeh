# Sound design

The picture is cold and quiet. The sound is what makes it land. There is no melody in this reel. A melody tells people what to feel. This piece is stronger when they decide.

## The spine

- **One note.** A low D drone, played once at frame 0 and sustained under the whole cold half of the reel. It disappears for the gym, comes back an octave lower at the pull back, and stops dead when the arc stops.
- **A pulse.** From shot 6 onward, a soft tick that accelerates like a clock in a time lapse. It is what makes the day feel like it is slipping.
- **One clean sound.** The tick when the render bar hits 100. It is the loudest, driest, most isolated sound in the reel. Everything else steps aside for it.

## Mix notes

- Deliver at minus 14 LUFS integrated, true peak minus 1 dB. Instagram normalises, so a loud master gains nothing and loses headroom.
- Put the gym at the loudest point in the mix. The rest of the reel sits 8 to 10 dB under it. That gap is the story.
- The 100 percent tick should read at roughly the same peak as the gym bass hits but with no low end. It is the same volume in a different register.
- No music library stems. If you use a drone from a library, pitch it to D and strip anything that moves.
- Check the mix on a phone speaker. Most viewers will hear it there. The drone will vanish on a phone. The tick, the clack and the whoosh must still carry the structure on their own.
