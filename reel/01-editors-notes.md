# Editor's notes

Your shot list is strong. The structure already does the hard thing, which is to say something true without saying it out loud. My job here is not to replace it. It is to make it survive contact with Instagram, where a viewer decides in under half a second, watches with the sound off half the time, and sees it loop.

These are the changes, in order of how much they matter.

## 1. Frame one is the whole reel

Instagram shows the first frame as the cover and as the thumbnail in the feed. A haze layer with nothing in it is a scroll past.

**Change:** do not start above the haze. Start already inside the fall, with the lit window visible at frame 0 as a single point of warm light in a field of dark ones. The camera is already moving. The bass hit lands on frame 0. Everything in the first 12 frames must say "something is happening and it is happening to that window".

## 2. Two still beats back to back

Shots 7 and 8 (nameplate, render bar) are both "nothing changes" beats. In a ten second piece, two consecutive stasis shots is where you lose people.

**Change:** keep both. They carry the meaning. But make them different kinds of still.

- Shot 7 is a cut on action. The nameplate slides in and stops with a clack. The camera pull back is the reveal that nothing else moved. It is a joke, told dry.
- Shot 8 is where the sound drops out. The bar crawls in near silence. The one typed command is the only sound. The snap to 100 percent is the cleanest, loudest single tick in the reel, and it is what launches the final shot. The stillness is the setup for the punch.

## 3. Never show the face

Shot 3 has "his face is a half reflection on the glass". Keep the reflection, but keep it as a shape, not a portrait.

Two reasons. First, AI video cannot hold one face across nine shots, and the moment a viewer sees two different men the piece breaks. Second, a man you never quite see is a man the viewer becomes. Anchor identity with two constants instead of a face: a dark charcoal overshirt and a watch on the left wrist. Those appear in shots 2, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9.

## 4. It loops. Cut for the loop.

A reel plays again the instant it ends. Your last frame is black and your first frame is haze. That is a dead beat on every loop.

**Change:** cut the black to 3 frames, or drop it. Frame 240 is the map, arc unlanded, city lights dim. Frame 0 is Cairo from above at night. Same visual grammar, so the loop reads as "and again", which is the point of the reel. On the second loop the viewer sees the lit window and now knows who is in it.

## 5. Anamorphic in a vertical frame

Anamorphic means wide. Instagram is tall. You cannot have both literally. What you can have is the anamorphic texture: oval bokeh, horizontal blue streak flares on practicals, a soft edge fall off. Ask for that and compose tall.

Shots 1, 3, 5 and 9 are naturally vertical (a fall, a corridor, a keeper alone, a map). Shot 2 needs a tighter orbit. Shot 4 needs the light shaft to be a vertical column so the frame has a spine.

## 6. Motion grammar

Cold shots move slowly and smoothly. Gym moves handheld and fast. That contrast is doing a lot of work, so protect it.

- Into the gym: the whip pan is the transition. Frame 81 (end of corridor) blurs horizontally into frame 82 (gym) in one continuous smear. Generate both shots with room to spare and build the whip in post with directional blur. Do not trust the generator to land a whip.
- Out of the gym: hard cut on the bar's downstroke into the keeper's crouch. Vertical motion into vertical motion. The eye never has to search.

## 7. Speed is the edit's friend

Every generator gives you 5 to 10 seconds per clip. You are using 1 to 1.4 seconds of each. That is a gift. Generate long, pick the best 30 frames, and ramp.

- Descent: run at 1.5x, ease out as it reaches the window.
- Orbit: 0.8x. Let it feel heavy.
- Time lapse: whatever the generator gives, then cut it to 28 frames.
- Final pull back: ramp from 1x to 2.5x as the window becomes the city.

## 8. On screen text

Only diegetic text. "Senior" on the nameplate. The inbox counter reading 0. The render bar reading 100 percent. No captions, no title, no name. The piece is stronger silent than explained.

If you want a caption for sound off viewers, put it in the post caption, not on the video.

## 9. What the ending means

"The arc stops before it lands" reads as "not yet", not "never". That is the right note. Do not soften it with a fade up or a title. The single addition I would make: the tip of the arc pulses once, faintly, at frame 236. It is still alive. Then hold.

## One thing this reel is, beyond a reel

You listed video editing as a skill you want. This is a rep. It is a portfolio asset that leaves with you regardless of what happens at the job, and it is exactly the kind of thing a European client watches and remembers. Log the hours, keep every version, and treat the finished file as proof of work, not just a post.
